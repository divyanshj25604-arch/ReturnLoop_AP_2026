## 1. System Overview & Domain Context

Retailers handling consumer electronics face a high volume of product returns resulting from shipping errors, minor defects, remorse returns, or damaged packaging. Each returned item cannot be treated uniformly; it must be routed to one of six possible dispositions:

1. **Resell**: Resell the product immediately as-is (e.g. open-box in excellent condition).
2. **Refurbish**: Repair, re-box, or sanitize the device to restore functional condition, then resell at secondary market value.
3. **Exchange**: Utilize the item as replacement inventory to fulfill ongoing warranty claims or direct customer exchanges.
4. **Donate**: Donate the item to educational, non-profit, or charitable organizations for community benefit and tax offset.
5. **Recycle**: Decommission non-repairable units to certified e-waste processors for responsible environmental and material recovery.
6. **Write-off**: Record the unit as an irrecoverable operational loss when processing or salvage costs exceed residual value.

The database architecture captures the end-to-end operational lifecycle: from initial product catalog definition and receiving at regional fulfillment centers, through multi-point technical inspection, algorithmic decision recommendations with evaluated alternatives, operator review/overrides, and final financial recovery outcomes.

## 2. Conceptual & Logical Database Entities

The relational architecture models eight fundamental entities designed to avoid data redundancy and maintain strict third normal form (3NF) compliance:

### 2.1 Products (`products`)
* **Description:** Represents master catalog specifications for consumer electronic models handled by the platform.
* **Role in Lifecycle:** Pure reference master. Contains SKU, brand, model name, category, and standard retail price (MSRP in INR).
* **Normalization Principle:** Isolates product attributes from individual return events, ensuring that changes to product pricing or categorization do not cause update anomalies across past returns.

### 2.2 Warehouses (`warehouses`)
* **Description:** Physical logistics facilities, intake hubs, and reverse-logistics fulfillment centers where returns arrive and undergo triage.
* **Role in Lifecycle:** Tracks facility code, facility name, geographic location (city, state), and intake holding capacity.

### 2.3 Operators (`operators`)
* **Description:** Warehouse staff, certified diagnostics technicians, and triage supervisors responsible for receiving, inspecting, and deciding dispositions.
* **Role in Lifecycle:** Employs audit traceability. Links each inspection and disposition override to a specific authenticated human operator.

### 2.4 Returns (`returns`)
* **Description:** Individual customer return events received at a designated warehouse for a specific catalog product.
* **Role in Lifecycle:** Represents the operational return unit tracked by a unique tracking number (`return_tracking_number`), optional device hardware serial number/IMEI, customer-reported return reason, arrival date, current processing status, and intake comments.

### 2.5 Inspections (`inspections`)
* **Description:** Structured technical assessments performed on returned items by certified operators upon intake.
* **Role in Lifecycle:** Evaluates cosmetic and hardware condition grade (`Excellent`, `Good`, `Fair`, `Damaged`), packaging completeness, accessory presence, functional hardware operational status, estimated repair cost (INR), and technician diagnostic notes.

### 2.6 Disposition Decisions (`disposition_decisions`)
* **Description:** The disposition determination reached for a returned item.
* **Role in Lifecycle:** Contains the algorithmic recommendation generated by the decision intelligence system, the final approved disposition, the operator who finalized the decision, recommendation confidence score, an override flag (`is_override`), and mandatory justification notes when an operator overrules the algorithmic advice.

### 2.7 Decision Alternatives (`decision_alternatives`)
* **Description:** Comparative evaluation matrix of alternative disposition channels evaluated during decision making.
* **Role in Lifecycle:** For a given decision, records candidate disposition pathways with estimated gross salvage revenue (INR), processing and repair costs (INR), net expected recovery, feasibility score, rank order, and recommendation status.

### 2.8 Decision Outcomes (`decision_outcomes`)
* **Description:** Post-execution actual financial and operational recovery metrics for completed dispositions.
* **Role in Lifecycle:** Validates decision quality by capturing executed disposition channel, actual liquidation/resale revenue realized (INR), actual repair/handling cost incurred (INR), actual net recovery, third-party liquidation/recycling partner, completion date, and audit notes.

## 4. Entity Relationships & Cardinalities

```
  +------------------+         +------------------+
  |     products     |         |    warehouses    |
  +------------------+         +------------------+
          | 1                          | 1        | 1
          |                            |          |
          | N                          | N        | N
  +------------------+                 |   +------------------+
  |     returns      |-----------------+   |    operators     |
  +------------------+                     +------------------+
       | 1        | 1                               | 1
       |          |                                 | (decides)
       | N        | 1                               |
+--------------+ +-----------------------+          |
| inspections  | | disposition_decisions |<---------+
+--------------+ +-----------------------+
                     | 1            | 1
                     |              |
                     | N            | 1
       +-----------------------+  +-----------------------+
       | decision_alternatives |  |   decision_outcomes   |
       +-----------------------+  +-----------------------+
```

### Detailed Cardinality Rules:

1. **`products` to `returns` (1 : N)**
   - **Semantics:** A catalog product can be returned multiple times across different customers and orders over time ($0 \dots N$). Each return references exactly one catalog product ($1 \dots 1$).
   - **Foreign Key:** `returns.product_id REFERENCES products(product_id) ON DELETE RESTRICT`.

2. **`warehouses` to `returns` (1 : N)**
   - **Semantics:** A warehouse receives and processes zero or many returns ($0 \dots N$). Each return is received at exactly one warehouse facility ($1 \dots 1$).
   - **Foreign Key:** `returns.warehouse_id REFERENCES warehouses(warehouse_id) ON DELETE RESTRICT`.

3. **`warehouses` to `operators` (1 : N)**
   - **Semantics:** A warehouse employs one or many operators ($1 \dots N$). Each operator has a primary home warehouse assignment ($1 \dots 1$).
   - **Foreign Key:** `operators.warehouse_id REFERENCES warehouses(warehouse_id) ON DELETE RESTRICT`.

4. **`returns` to `inspections` (1 : N)**
   - **Semantics:** A returned unit undergoes one or more technical inspection passes (e.g. initial triage, follow-up diagnostic) ($1 \dots N$). Each inspection record belongs to exactly one return ($1 \dots 1$).
   - **Foreign Key:** `inspections.return_id REFERENCES returns(return_id) ON DELETE CASCADE`.

5. **`operators` to `inspections` (1 : N)**
   - **Semantics:** An operator conducts zero or many technical inspections ($0 \dots N$). Each inspection record is certified by exactly one operator ($1 \dots 1$).
   - **Foreign Key:** `inspections.inspector_id REFERENCES operators(operator_id) ON DELETE RESTRICT`.

6. **`returns` to `disposition_decisions` (1 : 1)**
   - **Semantics:** A return reaches a single formalized disposition decision stage ($0 \dots 1$). Each decision corresponds to exactly one return unit ($1 \dots 1$). Enforced via `UNIQUE(return_id)`.
   - **Foreign Key:** `disposition_decisions.return_id REFERENCES returns(return_id) ON DELETE CASCADE`.

7. **`operators` to `disposition_decisions` (1 : N)**
   - **Semantics:** An operator or triage manager authorizes zero or many disposition decisions ($0 \dots N$). Each decision is authorized by exactly one operator ($1 \dots 1$).
   - **Foreign Key:** `disposition_decisions.decided_by_operator_id REFERENCES operators(operator_id) ON DELETE RESTRICT`.

8. **`disposition_decisions` to `decision_alternatives` (1 : N)**
   - **Semantics:** A disposition decision evaluates multiple candidate alternative channels ($1 \dots N$, typically 2 to 6). Each alternative record belongs to exactly one decision ($1 \dots 1$). Enforced via `UNIQUE(decision_id, disposition_type)` and `UNIQUE(decision_id, rank_order)`.
   - **Foreign Key:** `decision_alternatives.decision_id REFERENCES disposition_decisions(decision_id) ON DELETE CASCADE`.

9. **`disposition_decisions` to `decision_outcomes` (1 : 1)**
   - **Semantics:** Once an approved disposition is executed in the field, it produces at most one final financial/operational recovery outcome ($0 \dots 1$). Each outcome corresponds to exactly one finalized decision ($1 \dots 1$). Enforced via `UNIQUE(decision_id)`.
   - **Foreign Key:** `decision_outcomes.decision_id REFERENCES disposition_decisions(decision_id) ON DELETE RESTRICT`.

## 5. Functional Dependencies (FD Analysis)

To ensure strict compliance with Third Normal Form (3NF) and Boyce-Codd Normal Form (BCNF), the functional dependencies for each relation are formally defined below:

### 5.1 Relation: `products`
* Primary Key: `{product_id}`
* Alternate Key: `{sku}`
* Functional Dependencies:
  - $\text{FD}_{1.1}: \{\text{product\_id}\} \rightarrow \{\text{sku}, \text{brand}, \text{model\_name}, \text{category}, \text{retail\_price}, \text{created\_at}\}$
  - $\text{FD}_{1.2}: \{\text{sku}\} \rightarrow \{\text{product\_id}, \text{brand}, \text{model\_name}, \text{category}, \text{retail\_price}, \text{created\_at}\}$
* **Normal Form Evaluation:** Every determinant is a candidate key. No transitive or partial dependencies exist. (Satisfies 3NF / BCNF).

## 6. Suitability for Normalization & Normal Forms Checklist

This schema was purposefully engineered to facilitate Parth's normalization documentation (`01_normalization.md`):

1. **First Normal Form (1NF) Compliance:**
   - All attributes contain strictly atomic, scalar values (e.g. no comma-separated arrays for return reasons, packaging checklists, or alternative channels).
   - Every table possesses a defined unique primary key.
   - Repeating groups are decomposed into child entities (`decision_alternatives` rather than embedding multiple alternative columns into `disposition_decisions`).

2. **Second Normal Form (2NF) Compliance:**
   - 1NF is satisfied across all tables.
   - All non-key attributes are fully functionally dependent on the entire primary key. In relations with composite candidate keys (`decision_alternatives`), no partial functional dependency on `{decision_id}` or `{disposition_type}` exists.

3. **Third Normal Form (3NF) Compliance:**
   - 2NF is satisfied across all tables.
   - No transitive dependencies exist ($X \rightarrow Y$ and $Y \rightarrow Z$). Non-key attributes are directly dependent upon the candidate keys:
     - Retail price and product category reside only in `products`, never copied into `returns`.
     - Warehouse location details (`city`, `state`) reside strictly in `warehouses`, never repeated in `returns` or `operators`.
     - Inspection details (condition, repair costs) reside in `inspections`, not denormalized into `disposition_decisions`.
